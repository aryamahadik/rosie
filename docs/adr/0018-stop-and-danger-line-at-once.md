## Context

### CQ-230: a STOP batch behind a turn that has restarted

- **The lease's rule.** "A batch that closes before the running turn commits restarts that turn once, with its texts ... A batch that closes after that restart, or once the turn has begun its commit, waits; when the turn commits, every waiting batch opens the next turn together" (`runtime/src/actor/lease.ts` lines 11 to 13; `planOf`, lines 1111 to 1124, where `live.restart === 1` at line 1122 keeps every later batch waiting). It follows arch 02 line 113: "A text arriving before the commit restarts the turn once, with it (DD)". A lease lasts 60 s, a lapse reruns the turn, and its third lapse with no commit parks it (lines 79 to 82 and 1092 to 1109; arch 01 line 61; arch 11 line 576).
- **The wait.** A turn that cannot commit holds the lease to its third lapse. In CQ-230's case it holds an owed danger, which `resultsOf` leaves out, and no keyword text, so every run ends `noSafetyResult` (`runtime/src/turn/turn.ts` lines 436 to 437). A STOP batch that closes after its one restart waits all that time, about 3 minutes, then opens the next turn. Meanwhile the outbox can still send to the number: limit 8 drops a text to a stopped number only once the STOP's own commit has set the flag (arch 06 line 313), which the actor copies right after (`runtime/src/actor/handle-actor.ts` lines 719 to 746).
- **What binds.** RULES 7.2 (lines 312 to 313): "Unsure: treat it as a stop. It stops texts from the number it answers, at once." Arch 06 line 329: "Changes that only protect, such as a stop, stricter contact or a death report, commit at once." DL-508 ruled that a STOP never waits on the decided part, since a park records no STOP (K-05 v1 parkTurn carries no core actions), and T-1263 built it: a turn holding a stop word commits its keyword texts on the run that cannot make the decided part, with a result missing or not, and on the run whose decided request the gate refuses (turn.ts lines 9 to 18, 436 to 453 and 665 to 697; DL-521). ADR-015 line 105 took the lease's rule as it stands ("as the lease does today"). So a STOP that reaches a turn commits at once, and one that cannot reach it waits.
- **What the lease knows of a text.** Nothing of its words: it hands the turn its texts sealed (lease.ts line 170), and only the safety read, the turn and the PostgreSQL-down path open them (handle-actor.ts lines 51 to 52; turn.ts lines 6 to 7). That path already reads every text it follows against the actor's copy of the pinned bundle's keyword list, as "a STOP, a START, any other text, or `unknown` when there is no copy or its words cannot be opened (it may be a stop word: the stricter reading holds while it waits)" (`runtime/src/outage/db/outage.ts` lines 576 to 602), and the batch window's hand-off waits for that reading (lines 219 to 221 and 325 to 346). So, where that path is wired, every text it follows in a batch the lease takes has been read. Where it is not wired it reads nothing: `arrive` follows no text (lines 255 to 260) and `handOff` hands each batch on unread (lines 330 to 331). The composition root does not wire it yet: its `install()` sets the lease's hooks (`runtime/src/wiring/root.ts` line 667) and not `setDbOutageDeps` (outage.ts lines 96 to 105; root.ts lines 661 to 670), which D-017 is to join (`runtime/src/outage/db/index.ts` line 4).

### CQ-231: the direct danger line and its hold

- **The line's place.** RULES 4.12 (lines 245 to 246): "Danger now: the safety message first". Arch 02 line 111: "Any danger: the outcome step's checked message goes first". Arch 04 line 211: "Safety line, from arrival | ≤ 5 s".
- **What puts it off.** With the core not answering, `sendDanger` writes the hold again before it chooses and sends the line (`runtime/src/safety/arrival.ts` lines 1647 to 1667), and that write (`writeHold`, lines 1568 to 1596) is not guarded. A storage throw there leaves `sendDanger`; `applyResult`'s catch marks the line failed (lines 1483 to 1486), and the line goes only at the next re-read, 30 s on (line 1490). Every other write before the line is guarded: step 1's (lines 1440 to 1455), whose `holdFromOutcomes` writes the same hold with a guard (lines 1347 to 1366), the keep step (line 1457) and the noted line's (`noteLine`, lines 1695 to 1710; ADR-015 line 93, which left `writeHold` as it was).
- **What a missing hold binds today.** While a danger is pending with no open hold, `unreadTexts` names it (lines 703 to 705), so every commit holds every part but a protective one (`actions/src/commit/hold.ts` lines 182 to 184), as `holdFromOutcomes` promises: "the text then stays among the texts not read at commit time, and a resume or re-read writes the hold again" (lines 1351 to 1352). But `dangerSent` clears the pending note once the line has gone, the record is kept and the result is settled (lines 1312 to 1328), whether or not the hold was written. From then the text binds no commit through the actor: no hold, no note, its row done. Its record, queued for the core, keeps the lease's commits back until the core takes it (`beforeCommit`: lease.ts lines 681 to 683 and 918 to 925; outage.ts lines 348 to 356), and K-34's hold binds them after (`actions/src/tool-api/turn-face/commit.ts` lines 226 to 249); but only the lease's commit waits for that queue, and a clock row's commit is handed only the holds and the texts not read (handle-actor.ts line 288).

## Decision

### 1. A STOP batch restarts a restarted turn once more, and commits on that run (CQ-230)

**The rule.** A waiting batch that may hold a stop word (STOP or another opt-out word: arch 02 line 115) restarts the running turn a second time when that turn:

- is leased, so it has not begun its commit (lease.ts lines 684 to 691), is not holding its lease for its reply and is not being parked;
- has used its one restart; and
- is not left for the park (`stuck`, line 1121).

The lease replaces it as it does at the one restart, in one storage transaction that decides again from what is stored (lines 537 to 569): a turn of the running turn's texts and every waiting batch's, under the hash of their union, at try 1, the replaced turn marked replaced and its run told to stop (lines 540 to 543 and 571). The turn so opened is never restarted again: any later batch, a STOP's or not, waits for the next turn, which opens once that turn commits or parks, as today. So a turn is restarted at most twice, and the second time only for a batch that may hold a stop word (while the down path is unwired, any batch: below). `LeasedTurn.restart` and `TurnEntry.restart` read true for either restart (lines 629 and 1053).

**Why that is at once.** The turn the STOP batch opens holds the stop word, and a turn holding one commits its keyword texts on its first run whatever its decided part or a missing result does (T-1263, above), with the records it has, a text with no result named among the texts not read, and every routine part held (hold.ts lines 182 to 184). The STOP's `recordStopOrStart` so commits on that run, and its stopped flag is copied before that turn's reply goes (handle-actor.ts lines 719 to 746). A STOP whose batch takes the second restart waits for that batch to close (and, with the down path wired, be read), then for one run, never for the running turn's lapses. A STOP whose batch closes once the second restart has gone waits for the turn it opened to commit or park: seconds when that turn holds a stop word too, up to its third lapse only when it holds none and cannot commit (open point 1).

**Which batch may hold a stop word.** The lease asks its host, through a new optional `LeaseHost` member, `stopAmong(eventIds)`. The actor answers from the PostgreSQL-down path's reading of each text (outage.ts lines 576 to 602). Of a batch's texts it weighs those whose words are read (`isReadableEvent`, arrival.ts lines 312 to 318: a text, a caption, a vote's words, an edit): true when one of them reads as a STOP or `unknown`, or has no reading, not read yet or with no row (its `arrive` write threw: outage.ts lines 261 to 274), as RULES 7.2 says: "Unsure: treat it as a stop"; false when each reads as a START or another text (HELP among them), or the batch holds none (a tapback: no stop word is read in one). A reading that throws, or a text the actor cannot find, answers true, and the lease takes a throw from its host as true. A host without the member answers false, so a host built before this rule keeps today's; the actor's lease host (handle-actor.ts lines 262 to 281), the one this rule is for, gives it once Card S is in.

**While the down path is unwired, the host answers true** (round 2, DL-570, the lead's default; RULES 7.2's stricter reading: an unread batch may hold a STOP). Unwired, the path reads nothing (Context, above), so no batch the lease takes is known to hold no stop word, and that is the case wherever the composition root runs turns today. So, unwired, the first batch to close after a turn's one restart restarts it a second time, whatever it holds: a STOP in it commits at once, and a STOP in a later batch waits for the turn that restart opened to commit or park (open point 1). That is the whole cost: one more restart per turn, still bounded, since the turn so opened is never restarted again. `stopPending` (lines 297 to 313) answers false while unwired, as it does today: it asks whether a STOP the path has read waits untaken, and unwired it has read none; `stopAmong` asks whether a batch may hold one, and any unread batch may. With the path wired (D-017), the second restart goes only to a batch read as one that may hold a stop word.

**What does not change.**

- A turn that has begun its commit, holds its lease for its reply or is being parked is never restarted, for a STOP or not: its commit may have gone through (lines 669 to 676), and a restart could commit its texts under a second turn id. A STOP batch behind it waits for that commit's answer or that reply; only a core that keeps refusing or losing the commit, or a reply step that keeps crashing, stretches the wait to the third lapse (open point 2).
- While PostgreSQL is known down, no batch opens or restarts a turn (lines 532 to 533), and the actor takes a STOP itself at once (outage.ts lines 21 to 28; arch 11 line 566).
- A batch read as a START or HELP keeps the one restart (DL-508: neither only protects); while the down path is unwired no batch is read, so such a batch may take the second (the cost above).
- One lease row, a batch taken once, a replaced turn never committing (line 687), the turn order (`lease_turns.seq`, DL-427) and the PostgreSQL-down hold stay as they are. ADR-015's `closeResults` runs at no restart (its line 73), so the texts move with their kept results.

This amends ADR-015 line 105: a later batch restarts the running turn once with its texts, and a batch that may hold a stop word restarts it once more.

### 2. The direct danger line never waits for its hold, and every routine part stays held until it is written (CQ-231)

**The guard.** The direct path writes its hold through `holdFromOutcomes` (arrival.ts lines 1347 to 1366): the rule and the booking block it writes today (lines 1648 to 1656), never looser than the open hold, but guarded. A storage that throws there stops no line: the line this application chose, or the one noted for the text, goes on that application, within arch 04 line 211's 5 s, never at the next re-read for the hold's sake. Its words, its key (line 1778) and its record are unchanged. This amends ADR-015 line 93 ("this record leaves that write as it is"). With it, no write before a danger's direct line can stop it.

**The hold's absence binds every commit until the hold is written.** A danger is complete once its line has gone (or none is needed), its record is kept, its result is settled (ADR-015 section 2) and its hold is written, that is, its hold row holds an outcome's rule (any but `unread`), released or not; a read that throws counts as not written. Until then its pending note stays. So, with no open hold, it is among `unreadTexts` (lines 703 to 705), which the lease's commits and the clock rows' are handed alike (handle-actor.ts lines 270 and 288), and every part but a protective one is held (hold.ts lines 182 to 184; turn-face/commit.ts line 249), the safety flow included, which is stricter than its hold will be; `routineHeld` is true (line 722); and it is read again every 30 s (`waiting`, lines 589 to 591). The hold is written again:

1. **At once:** `dangerSent` (lines 1318 to 1328) first writes a missing hold from the labels kept for the text (`holdFromKept`, lines 1368 to 1381), then clears the note only when the result is settled and the hold is written; otherwise it notes the line done (`safety_line_done`) and the note stays.
2. **At the re-read or the resume:** for a pending danger whose line and record are done and whose result is settled, `rereadDue` (lines 523 to 542) and `resumeCutOff` (lines 480 to 491) write a missing hold the same way before they send any read, and `applyLate` does so after its own `holdFromKept` (line 1112). Once the hold is written the danger is complete: its note clears, no read is sent and no answer is used. The danger is never applied again for its hold, so no second line or core call comes of it.
3. **At the turn's end:** `closeResults` (lines 790 to 796) clears a line-done text's note only once its hold is written; the next re-read writes it and clears the note.
4. **At the record's replay:** when the queued record replays and the actor holds no outcome hold for the text, the core's hold is written as the actor's, with its id, rule and booking block (`writeHold`, guarded), where `replayOnReturn` today only sets the id on a row that has none (lines 911 to 917). So whichever comes first, the re-read's hold or the replay, the actor's hold carries the core's id once the core holds it, and `markHoldReleased` can find it (lines 838 to 850). Without this, a hold the re-read wrote after the replay would have no id for good, and every commit counts such a hold as a text not read (turn-face/commit.ts lines 35 to 37), after the core has released its own.
5. **`holdFromKept` fills only a missing hold:** it writes one only for a text with no outcome hold at all, released or not. Today it looks for an open one (lines 1374 to 1377), so a re-read of a pending danger whose hold the core has released (`releaseAtOnce`, lines 1638 and 1786 to 1806, or `markHoldReleased`) would open it again, with no id. A released hold stays released.

If the labels row could not be written either, no hold can be built from it: the text stays pending and unread, read again every 30 s, until a read that finds danger applies it again, and that application's step 1 writes both (open point 4).

**What does not change: the outbox path's `writeHold`** (lines 1635 to 1636). No line waits on it: the core already holds the line's outbox row. A throw there skips `dangerSent` and the at-once release (lines 1637 to 1638), so the danger stays pending and binds every commit, through its step-1 hold, which has no id (turn-face/commit.ts lines 35 to 37), or with none as a text not read (lines 703 to 705); and its next application that finds danger writes the core's hold with its id and releases it if its rule is at once. Guarded, a throw would lose that id, since a hold built from the labels has none, and skip the release, leaving K-34's hold and the actor's open for good (open point 3).

## Consequences

- A STOP commits on the first run of the turn that takes it, behind a running turn that has restarted too, whatever that turn could not decide or record, whenever the run reaches its keyword texts (the directory, the key store and the guard answering: turn.ts lines 347 to 376) and the core answers its commit. The number is stopped seconds after the STOP's batch closes, not minutes, unless the turn's second restart has already gone to a batch that held no stop word: any batch while the down path is unwired, a misread one while it is wired. That STOP waits for the turn the second restart opened to commit or park (open point 1).
- A turn is restarted at most twice, the second time only for a batch that may hold a stop word, which while the down path is unwired is any batch. One turn at a time per handle, one commit per turn id and the PostgreSQL-down hold stand.
- A trace can hold three batch spans, one per turn id, where CQ-217 (c) noted two.
- A danger's direct line goes on the application that finds it, whatever the storage does to its hold. A danger whose hold is missing holds every routine part of the handle, in every commit, until its hold is written: at the same application if the storage recovers at once, else at its next re-read 30 s on or its record's replay (open point 4 for labels lost as well).
- The actor's hold carries the core's id whichever comes first, its re-read or its record's replay, and a re-read never opens a released hold again.
- ADR-017's row for `safety_pending` (line 90: "while the danger is not complete") now takes in the hold, and its row for `safety_line_done` (line 94: "while the danger is pending and its result unsettled") also holds while the hold is missing. Its retention rule does not change: a pending danger keeps its text unsettled (its section 2, condition 2, line 56).

## Alternatives not chosen

- **The bounded wait stands:** RULES 7.2 says "at once", arch 06 line 329 commits a change that only protects at once, and DL-508 ruled that a STOP never waits on the decided part. A running turn's missing result or failing decided part is such a wait, and the outbox can send to the number meanwhile.
- **A STOP batch starts its own turn beside the running one:** two leases for one handle, against the lease's one row (lease.ts lines 397 to 404; arch 01 line 61: the handle's lock for one turn), and commits that could land in either order, against the turn order the actor's copies weigh answers by (DL-427).
- **The running turn pauses for the STOP's own turn:** the later text would commit first, against `lease_turns.seq`'s order, through a paused state the lease does not have.
- **The actor takes the STOP itself, as while PostgreSQL is down:** its reply would go straight through Linq, past the outbox and limit 8, and its record as a queued write with caller actorOnReturn while the core answers, beside the turn's own path; the down path's order rules (outage.ts lines 622 to 628) were made for an outage. The second restart reuses the turn's own path.
- **A run that ends without a commit reruns at once, not at the lapse:** its three tries would go in seconds, parking a turn that a re-read 30 s on might have let commit (an owed result kept), and the STOP would still wait for the park.
- **Restarting for any batch, any number of times:** a turn could restart for as long as texts keep coming. The second restart is bounded, and only for a batch that may hold a stop word.
- **Card S waits for D-017 to wire the down path, the host answering false until then** (round 2's other way): the composition root sets the lease's hooks and not the down path's (root.ts lines 661 to 670), each for its isolate (lease.ts lines 316 to 325; outage.ts lines 96 to 105), so wherever it runs turns the wait would stand, and the STOP's at-once would hang on the order other cards join their ports in, with nothing in the lease to show it. RULES 7.2 reads an unread batch as one that may hold a stop, at the cost of one bounded restart.
- **Restarting a turn that has begun its commit:** its commit may have gone through, so its texts could commit under a second turn id.
- **The direct line's delay stands:** the hold write adds nothing the line needs (its words come from the bundle or the noted line), step 1 has already tried the same write, and a safety line 30 s late for a bookkeeping write breaks arch 04 line 211's 5 s.
- **Guarding the write and letting the note clear as today:** a danger whose hold is missing would bind no commit through the actor once its line had gone, and a clock row's commit, which does not wait for the queue, could commit a routine part before the core holds the danger's hold.
- **A stand-in hold when the labels are missing** (the strictest, a threat's): a hold never released, on a guess. The text stays pending and unread until a read says what it is.
- **Guarding the outbox path's write too:** section 2, "What does not change".

## Open points (proposed for QUESTIONS.md, each with its default)

1. **A batch that may hold a stop word but holds none takes the turn's second restart.** With the down path wired that is a misread: the actor's copy of the keyword list can lag the bundle a turn pins (it is refreshed at each pin: handle-actor.ts lines 702 to 708), so a text it reads as a stop, or as `unknown` (no copy yet, or words that cannot be opened), may be none to the turn's guard; a STOP the copy reads as another text waits as before.
