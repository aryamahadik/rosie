# ADR-015: Where the arrival safety read's result is kept until its turn commits

- Status: proposed, default in use (T-1241; the D-017 path audit's ruling R3, DL-492)
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Owner: architect. Decides: the CTO.
- Contracts: none changes. K-02 v1 `safetyResult` is kept and handed on as the read gave it; K-05, K-06, K-08, K-09 and K-34 stay as they are.

## Context

- **What the commit needs.** Each text's outcome, No included, is a `recordSafetyOutcome` core action of the turn its text joins, since K-34 v1 writes one outcome row per text with that turn's id (K-09 README default 5; `safety_outcomes_text_has_handle_and_turn`, `db/migrations/0016_safety_incidents.sql` lines 81 to 83; `actions/src/domain/safety/record.ts` lines 311 to 326 refuse a text's record outside its turn). The request carries the text's whole K-02 v1 `safetyResult` (`runtime/src/turn/turn.ts` lines 557 to 574). The turn gets it from `resultsOf` (`runtime/src/turn/types.ts` lines 639 to 645: "The actor keeps only labels (runtime/src/safety), so the store behind this joins at D-017"), once per run, after the guard (turn.ts lines 368 to 373 and 543 to 551).
- **What the actor keeps today** (read on main 58acd53; T-1108 moved none of the lines named here). The safety read applies each result in one place, `applyResult` (`runtime/src/safety/arrival.ts` lines 1146 to 1222), and keeps its outcome labels only (lines 253 to 257 and 1163 to 1174: "Labels only, never the read's evidence words"). The whole result is kept nowhere, except a danger's record queued, sealed under the handle's key, while the core does not answer (lines 253 to 254 and 1266 to 1293). Staging binds `resultsOf` closed (`runtime/src/wiring/closed.ts` line 53; `picks.ts` line 95), and the test rig answers it from the safety double's own answers (`runtime/src/wiring/test-support.ts` lines 4305 to 4315), so no test yet shows a result the actor kept reaching a commit.
- **What a text with no result does today**, read again for this record. The audit's chain holds for the commit, but the run stops before it:
  - a turn that decides anything ends its run before the frontier when one of its readable texts has no result (`noSafetyResult`, turn.ts lines 423 to 426): nothing is decided or committed, the lease reruns the turn at each 60 s lapse and parks it after the third (`runtime/src/actor/lease.ts` lines 17 to 33; T-253). Once the turn's directory answers (T-1240's party read), every run of T-570's JOIN ends there while no result is kept, and the person hears once that nothing changed instead of S1-02's question. Neither audit report names this line (audit_actor-entries.txt part a item 3; order_d017.txt line 8);
  - a turn that decides nothing (keyword texts alone, or only events K-02 has no text for) leaves the text out of its records (turn.ts lines 553 to 574) and commits;
  - in the commit, the openHolds port names each of the turn's texts whose record is not among the core actions (`actions/src/tool-api/turn-face/commit.ts` lines 192 to 201 and 231 to 232), `readOpenHolds` lists each that K-34 holds no row for as unread (`db/repo/turn-face-reads.ts` lines 324 to 340), and while any is unread every part but a protective one is held (`actions/src/commit/hold.ts` lines 182 to 184).
- **What the result holds** (K-02 v1 `safetyLabel` and `safetyResult`, `contracts/model/model.v1.schema.json` lines 1453 to 1595): labels (outcome and tense), the model tries, the timeout marker and what holds after code's rules, and the person's words: each label's evidence words ("The words of the text that show it"), who is at risk and who wrote it, and the danger words code matched. K-34 keeps the labels plain and seals those words under the person's key (`sealed_words`: 0016 lines 16 to 21; K-34 README line 29 and rule 6; record.ts default 7).
- **Where a turn runs.** One actor per handle (arch 10 line 502), with single-writer storage; a turn runs in its actor's instance, but `TurnDeps`, where `resultsOf` sits today, is set once per Worker isolate for every actor in it (turn.ts lines 112 to 121; `runtime/src/wiring/root.ts` lines 467 to 470 and 579 to 593).
- **The audit's ruling R3** (order_d017.txt line 75; DL-492): "the result kept sealed in the actor's own storage. Reason: the commit must record the very result the actor read, with no second model call and no unsealed evidence words."

## Decision

### 1. Where: in the handle actor's own storage, sealed

- The actor keeps each text's K-02 result in a table of its own SQLite storage, `safety_results` (`event_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY`, `box TEXT NOT NULL`), made with the safety read's other tables (arrival.ts lines 231 to 264). One row per text.
- **Sealed:** the whole K-02 v1 `safetyResult` the read gave, as one JSON text, sealed as `sealWords` seals (`runtime/src/gateway/seal.ts` lines 121 to 136: AES-256-GCM, a fresh IV per seal) under the handle's sealing key (QUESTIONS.md NQ-RT-1's default; K-05 v1.2 item 29), the key the text's own words are sealed under. Its additional data names the actor and `result:<event id>`, as the write-on-return queue names `return:<key>` (arrival.ts lines 1280 to 1286), so a box opens only as its own text's result: never as a text's words, under another event id or in another actor.
- **Unsealed:** the event id alone (K-06's id: a label, never words). Everything of the result is inside the box, and these must be: each label's evidence words, who is at risk and who wrote it, and the danger words matched, the words K-34 seals (0016 lines 16 to 21; arch 01 line 93, sealed: "Encrypted under a per-person key"). K-02 v1 gives the model no other reasons field. The read's model calls (tokens, cost, time) are not part of the result and are not kept here; the safety read's span holds them as labels and counts (arrival.ts lines 1493 to 1532). The actor's labels table stays labels only (arrival.ts lines 255 to 257).
- **Nowhere else** between the read and the commit: not in PostgreSQL, KV or R2; not in a span, log line, error or event-log value (K-08 README line 79; arrival.ts lines 50 to 51; `runtime/src/safety/types.ts` line 14); not in decide's context. The turn hands it only to its commit's `recordSafetyOutcome` (turn.ts lines 557 to 574), and the commit seals the words into K-34 under the person's key, as it does today.
- **How the turn reaches it:** each run of a turn is handed its actor's results read with its leased turn, as it is handed the holds open at its start (lease.ts lines 207 to 212 and 652 to 654; `runtime/src/actor/handle-actor.ts` line 269), and the turn's safety step reads it there. `resultsOf` keeps its name and its answer, a list of K-02 v1 results. The isolate-wide port goes: `TurnDeps.safety` (types.ts lines 639 to 645 and 652) and `Ports.stateReads.turn.safety` (`runtime/src/wiring/ports.ts` lines 137 to 142; closed.ts lines 51 to 54; root.ts line 470).

Why here:

- The commit records the very result the actor read and acted on (its line, its hold and its labels came from it), with no second model call: the actor is where the read runs and its result is applied.
- The actor's storage outlives an eviction (T-1088's premise) and needs no database (arch 11 line 566), and it holds the sealed texts the turn reruns from (arch 01 line 61; arch 02 lines 109 and 113; arch 11 line 576), so a rerun reads the same results.
- Sealed under the handle's key, because the evidence words are the person's own words and who is at risk and who wrote it name people. Whatever keeps a person's words keeps them sealed under a per-person key, so that deleting the key erases them (arch 01 line 93; arch 09 line 492; arch 10 line 516; RULES 4.10; RULES 4.5: "When unsure, treat it as private"; K-34 rule 6). The handle's key is that key in the actor (NQ-RT-1: made at first sight and linked to the person once known), and it is the text's own key, so one deletion erases a text and its kept result together. The actor already keeps the read's evidence words this way while the core is down (arrival.ts lines 253 to 254).
- It is no second system of record (arch 10 line 504): a row lives only until the commit has written K-34's, or until its text can reach no commit (section 2), as the inbox holds a text for its turn.
- Through the leased turn, because the store is one actor's and `TurnDeps` is every actor's in the isolate: no isolate-wide port can reach one actor's storage without a registry of live actor instances by name, module state shared across Durable Object instances, which could hand one handle's results to another.

### 2. Its life

**Written** by `applyResult`, for every result it applies: on time, by the timeout rule, late, or on a re-read (arrival.ts lines 866 to 921, 948 to 1021 and 1146 to 1222).

- In the step that applies it, beside the text's labels row (lines 1170 to 1174), and before the text can count as read: before `finish` (lines 1218 to 1220), and before the late path releases the unread hold (lines 1011 to 1016). So no batch reaches the lease holding a text whose result is not kept (`unreadAmong`, lines 591 to 617).
- Sealed under the key the read opened the text's words with (`queueRead`, lines 828 to 864), held in memory with that read and never stored: keeping the result adds no key call and no network call to the path of a danger's line, which goes first (RULES 4.12), within 5 s of arrival (arch 04 line 211) and while PostgreSQL is down (arch 11 line 566).
- Keeping it never stops a line the result calls for and never holds one for anything outside the isolate, as no failed storage write may stop it (lines 1040 to 1065). A result that cannot be kept fails closed: the text does not count as read, so its batch stays back and routine work stays held, and the text is read again as a text whose read gave no verdict is (T-1088; DL-127). No text with a result counts as read before that result is kept.
- Not written for a text whose words open to nothing, which has no result (lines 848 and 882 to 885; open point 1), nor for the timeout stand-in of a text whose words could not be opened (`unreadable`, lines 833 to 847 and 895 to 907), which has no key to be sealed under. The text that could not be opened keeps its unread hold (lines 1195 to 1204) and is read again every 30 s (lines 469 to 496) until a read opens its words, and that read's verdict is kept.

**Replaced** each time the actor applies another result for the same text: the late verdict after a timeout stand-in (lines 948 to 1021), or a danger applied again after a cut or a failed line (lines 434 to 445 and 978 to 993). The row always holds the result the actor applied last, written in the step that writes the text's labels row again (lines 1163 to 1174), as the write-on-return queue keeps the newer box of the same record (lines 1266 to 1271). An answer the actor does not use never replaces it: a re-read with no verdict (lines 994 to 1002), any answer but a danger while a danger is pending ("the first verdict stands": lines 957 and 978 to 993), or a second answer while one lands (line 968).

**Read** by each run of the turn that carries the text, once, after the guard and before the frontier (turn.ts lines 368 to 373 and 543 to 551), opened with the handle's key (the safety read's keys port, the same sealing port as the turn's: root.ts lines 469 and 502). A restart's run, a lapse's rerun and a rerun after the commit read it again.

**Removed** when its text can no longer reach a commit, and only then:

- the turn that carries it is done: its lease released for good after its commit (the reply ended, `replied`, lease.ts lines 765 to 778; committed with no reply, `finish`, lines 784 to 804; the reply given up at its third lapse, lines 892 to 893), or the turn parked (the park answered parked or alreadyCommitted, lines 968 to 971; K-05 answered turnParked, lines 716 to 717). The rows of the turn's event ids go in the same storage transaction that ends the turn, so a crash leaves both or neither;
- or the PostgreSQL-down path took the text alone: a STOP or START it took never joins a turn (`runtime/src/outage/db/outage.ts` lines 21 to 28 and 325 to 346), so its row goes when the batch is handed on without it (handle-actor.ts lines 679 to 683).

Never removed by the commit's answer while its turn keeps the lease for the reply (lease.ts lines 735 to 759): a crash before the reply ends reruns the turn, and that rerun must read the same results to reach its repeated commit (arch 11 line 576). Nor by a restart (lines 540 to 543: the texts move to the restart's turn), a lapse's rerun, a turn held while PostgreSQL is down (T-270) or an eviction. The actor's labels rows and holds are not touched: they serve the holds (CQ-65 as DL-119 replaced it).

**What a crash between the read and the commit leaves:**

- Before the result is applied (the read in flight, or its answer still in memory): no row and no labels. The text is still being read, and the next instance resumes it (arrival.ts lines 404 to 467); the result that then lands is kept. The lost answer was never acted on (no line, hold or label came from it), so no record needs it.
- After it is kept, before the text counts as read (a danger's line under way): the row and its labels. A danger whose line or record is not done is read again, and a danger it then applies, with the same idempotency key (DL-132), replaces the row; any other text is resumed as above.
- After the text counted as read, before the commit: the row. The lease's lapse reruns the turn from its sealed texts, the rerun reads the same row and the commit records the very result; a third lapse parks the turn and removes the row.
- After the commit, before the reply ends: the row. The rerun reads it, its commit answers repeated with nothing written (K-05 v1 commitTurn, item 1: "whatever the repeat's body"), and only rows with no plan are composed; the row goes when the reply ends or is given up.
- In the step that ends the turn: both or neither, in one storage transaction.
- PostgreSQL down at any point: the row waits with the held turn (T-270); nothing here needs the database.

In no case does a text with a result count as read before that result is kept, and no commit records a result the actor did not apply.

### 3. What `resultsOf` answers, and a missing result

- For each asked event id with a row whose box opens under the handle's key to a K-02 result naming that same event id: that result, as kept.
- For any other id (no row; a box that does not open or parse; a result naming another event): nothing. The list leaves it out. Never a made-up result, a stand-in, a No by default, another text's result or a second read.
- A read that cannot be done at all (the key store or the storage throws) throws, and the turn takes that as no results (turn.ts lines 368 to 373).

So a missing result never lets a routine part commit unrecorded:

- In the runtime, a turn that decides anything ends its run at turn.ts lines 423 to 426 (`noSafetyResult`): no frontier, no decide and no gate. It reruns at each lapse and parks after the third (T-253), and the person hears once that nothing changed (arch 11 line 576).
- In the commit, for any request that reaches it without a text's record: that text is named to `readOpenHolds` and listed unread while K-34 holds no row for it (turn-face/commit.ts lines 231 to 232; turn-face-reads.ts lines 324 to 340), and every routine part is held before the gate (hold.ts lines 182 to 184 and 194 to 204).
- Only a change that only protects commits past a missing record (hold.ts line 182; arch 06 line 329; RULES 7.2: a stop works "at once"). A turn of keyword texts alone still commits its STOP or START with that text left out of its records (turn.ts lines 553 to 574), as it does today (open point 2).

### 4. No contract changes

- K-02 v1 `safetyResult`: kept and handed on as the read gave it.
- K-09: v1's `recordSafetyOutcome` and v1.5's `gateAndCommit` are unchanged; the turn already sends each text's result as a core action (default 5).
- K-05: no new call and no new row. `commitTurn`, `readOpenHolds` (v1.3) and `readSafetyOutcomeOfEvent` (v1.4) are used as they are, and T-1240's minor version names no safety-result read (its card's "not here").
- K-34 v1: the commit writes `safety_outcomes` and `sealed_words` as today.
- K-06 v1: the row is keyed by the event id only. K-08 v1: no new span, kind or value.
- The actor's storage layout is not a contract: its tables are the runtime's own (arrival.ts lines 231 to 264; `runtime/src/actor/inbox.ts`; lease.ts), and 02-contracts lists none. `TurnDeps`, `LeasedTurn` and `Ports` are the runtime's hand-written shapes (types.ts lines 1 to 3; ports.ts lines 3 to 5), changed inside the runtime engineer's cards below. So no version card is needed, and no contract test changes.

## Consequences

- The commit records the very K-02 result the actor read and acted on, with no second model call, and the person's words in it are sealed under a per-person key from the read to K-34: in the actor under the handle's key, in K-34 under the person's.
- With the party read and both cards, T-570's JOIN finds its No result in its actor, and its commit writes the text's outcome row (K-05 item 37: `read-safety-outcome-of-event-s1-01` finds none before the commit).
- The actor holds one sealed row per text in flight, only until its turn is done; it never outlives its text, and deleting the handle's key erases both together.
- The hop rig's results come from the real arrival path once card B lands, so the hop and skeleton tests prove the store end to end instead of a double's second answer.
- `resultsOf` leaves the root's ports: the state-reads card is smaller, and T-1242's `resultsOf` seam is superseded.
- Not changed here: the danger path's own record on arrival (K-09 v1.1 default 36, journey 3's lane). T-246 still sends it as a job with no turn, which the action layer refuses (K-09 README line 392; `textNeedsTurn`). When it is built, a danger text's batch-turn record answers repeated (README line 394), and the kept result still rides as that core action.
- A parked turn's texts get no K-34 outcome row, since K-05 v1 parkTurn carries no core actions (types.ts lines 563 to 572), and their kept results go with the park; K-37's `parked_turns` keeps no value from a turn's texts (its README line 27).

## Alternatives not chosen

- **A K-05 row written at arrival** (a new table, or K-34's row before the turn): K-34 holds a text's outcome to a turn (0016 lines 81 to 83) and K-09 v1.1 refuses an arrival record of No (README line 395; `refuse-j3-arrival-record-of-no.json`), so it needs a migration and a minor version for a second home of one fact. It puts a database write on the arrival path, where the danger line must not wait (arch 04 line 211) and which must work while PostgreSQL is down (arch 11 line 566), and a write outside the turn between `readOpenHolds` and `commitTurn` (K-05 open gap 32). With no person known, K-34's key is not there to seal under (K-34 README line 29).
- **Every text recorded on arrival in its own arrival turn** (K-09 v1.1's `actorOnArrival` widened to No): K-09 keeps No out on purpose (default 36), it costs a K-05 transaction per text on the arrival path, it writes K-34's row under the arrival turn instead of the turn the text joins (default 5), and it does nothing while PostgreSQL is down.
- **Reading the text again for the commit** (a second model call): the commit must record the result the actor acted on; a second read can differ, costs a model call per text per run and up to 4 s, and fails while the AI provider is down (arch 11 line 564), so a record could contradict the hold the actor wrote.
- **Memory only:** an eviction loses it (T-1088), and a rerun after a lapse (arch 11 line 576) must read the same result.
- **The actor's storage unsealed, or labels only:** unsealed keeps a person's words outside a per-person key (arch 01 line 93; RULES 4.10). Labels only is not the result K-09 takes (K-02 requires the words, and K-34 seals them), and rebuilding a result from labels makes one up.
- **A column on the inbox row:** the inbox keeps the event as the gateway handed it and never opens a box (`runtime/src/actor/inbox.ts` lines 6 to 7; handle-actor.ts lines 51 to 53), and a result is replaced and removed on its own life while the text is kept.
- **KV or R2:** not transactional with the actor's storage, eventually consistent (KV takes about 60 s: arch 10 line 518), and one more place for a person's words.
- **An isolate-wide `resultsOf` bound by the root** (as ports.ts lines 25 to 27 planned, through K-05): an isolate serves many actors, and reaching one actor's storage needs a registry of live actor instances, module state shared across Durable Objects (the audit quotes Cloudflare's in-memory-state page: "it is best to avoid global variables") that could hand one handle's results to another. The leased turn already carries the actor's own state, its texts and its holds.

## Open points (proposed for QUESTIONS.md, each with its default)

1. **A text whose words open to nothing** (an empty or spaces-only body or subject, which the gateway seals: `runtime/src/gateway/hand-off.ts` lines 187 to 198; the read finds nothing: arrival.ts lines 848 and 882 to 885) has no K-02 result, so a turn that decides it ends `noSafetyResult` on every run and parks. Default: unchanged, failing closed. To rule: whether such a text needs a record at all (K-02 has no shape for nothing to read; K-34 holds a row per read text). Owner: the architect with the runtime engineer.
2. **A turn of keyword texts alone whose text has no kept result** commits its STOP or START with that text left out, so K-34 never holds that text's outcome. Default: unchanged (a stop never waits: RULES 7.2; arch 06 line 329).
3. **A danger applied again replaces the kept result**, so when two danger reads of one text name different outcomes the commit records the last, and who is told follows it (record.ts), while the actor's hold stays the strictest of all its reads (arrival.ts lines 1295 to 1323) and binds the commit beside K-34's (turn-face/commit.ts defaults 1 and 2). Default: the last applied, as the write-on-return queue keeps the newer box. Owner: the architect with the security reviewer.
4. **A text read before card A is deployed** has no row, so a turn that decides it parks once (fails closed). Default: accepted; no staging turn runs today (the turn's directory is closed: closed.ts line 52), and production runs nothing.
