### Which part owns what

One fact lives in one place: this part names other parts' rows by their keys and repeats none of their columns.

- **K-04 core** (frozen) owns the booking head (booker, person served, payer, stage, version), persons, handles, phones, open questions (the owner's fee and refund questions), outbox rows (a plan charge's reminder), turns and the event log. Nothing here repeats a head column; the head's `payer_person_id` is who the rest is billed to, and `payments.payer_person_id` is who actually paid each payment (RULES 3.13).
- **K-07 outbox and effect rows** (frozen, #77) owns every Stripe call's effect row: its effect id, its one idempotency key, tries, runs, state, the provider's object id and the ledger line it writes or matches (`effects.ledger_line_id`, no foreign key in K-07 v1). A payment, a refund and a card save each name exactly one effect (unique), so a retried or re-run effect never makes a second row. **K-05 v1.1** writes the effect results and provider events that move these rows.
- **K-14 Stripe port** (frozen): the calls, results and events, and the forms of Stripe's ids. Nothing of a card but Stripe's ids is stored here; the brand and last four are read live with `pageIntent` and `savedCard` (K-14 v1 README "Money and cards"), never stored. A pay link is K-14's `payLink` payment on Rosie's own pay page; `pay_links` keeps the link itself (only its code's hash, as K-30's secure links do), and the page's client secret is read from Stripe by the payment's effect, never stored (K-14 v1 README "Pay links"). K-30's secure links take no pay link (their purposes are the secure page, photos and quotes). K-14's purposes are `payments.purpose` (in snake case, without Rosfield's monthly bill), and its `refund.reason` follows `refunds.reason` (`duplicate` for `double_charge` and `second_payment`, `requestedByCustomer` for every other, `unbound_charge` included: K-14 v1 has no other reason, open gaps). How each K-14 result and event fills these rows is the section "K-14's results and events, row by row" below.
- **K-25 services and prices** (frozen) owns services and ways; a pack and a membership name a service of their business by `services (id, business_id)`.
- **K-28 customers** (frozen) owns the customer record; a pack and a membership name it by `customers (business_id, person_id)` (`customers_business_person_key`), so a foreign key holds them to a customer of their own business (K-28 README line 29: "K-32 money names a customer by customers.id", read this way: default 9).
- **K-29 conversations and consent** (frozen) owns every yes: a plan's own yes to its terms is its `consents` row of kind `plan_terms`, named by `memberships.terms_consent_id`, never copied; the words ticked for a booking's money are K-30's terms snapshot's.
- **K-30 bookings and changes** (frozen, #120) owns the booking line with its price, deposit and fee copied in and `paid_by` (money or credit), and the visits. Payments, refunds and credits name `booking_lines.id`; a fee and a $2 name `booking_visits (id, booking_id)`. The booking's one fee amount is worked out from its open lines' copies (K-30 README "Money").
- **K-33 platform billing** (frozen, #92) owns Rosfield's monthly bill, its lines, the bill card and the pause; each bill line names its fee here by `monthly_bill_lines.fee_id` (`rosfield_fees.id`). The foreign key belongs to K-33's table, so it is a K-33 minor version (open gaps). Rosfield's own charges, pay links and their reconciliation are K-33's and K-07's; nothing here is Rosfield's own money.
- **K-31 the visit** (F17, not frozen) will own check-ins, DONE and prices and extras set at the visit; a $2 is counted here at the first check-in code or DONE that K-31 records.
- **K-23 business setup** (frozen) owns the business's Stripe account id (`business_setup.stripe_account_id`), which every K-14 call on the business's account takes.

### K-14's results and events, row by row

Each K-14 call runs from its K-07 effect row, written in the same commit as the money row it fills; the effect's result and the provider events (K-05 v1.1, the runtime side) then set that row's set-once values. A Stripe payment becomes paid only this way (rule 5).

- **`saveCard`**: the commit writes the `saved_cards` row with its `stripe_save_card` effect and the port request's `customerId`, which is null on a person's first save on that account ("null makes one, and the response names it"); the result's `customerId` then sets `stripe_customer_id` once. `cardSaved` sets `stripe_payment_method_id` and `saved_at` once (and the customer id, if it is still null); a card is never saved without its customer (`saved_cards_saved_with_method`). `cardReplaced` sets `replaced_at`; `removeCard` sets `removed_at` and `removed_why`.
- **`charge`**: the commit writes the payment `pending` with its effect (and, for a fee or a plan charge, naming it). `charged`: `paid_at` from `chargedAt`, a ledger charge line with the `chargeId`, and the fee's `charged_at` or the plan charge's `charged_at`. `needsAuthentication`: the payment stays `pending` with no line until `paymentSucceeded` or `paymentFailed` (K-14 README "Effects"). A decline: `failed`, `declined`, never retried (RULES 3.11); a fee stays `owner_yes` and a plan charge is `failed`, each with its one pay link to follow. An effect let go after an outage (K-07 `letGo`; S12-17): `cancelled`.
- **`payLink`**: the commit writes the payment `pending` with its `stripe_pay_link` effect and its `pay_links` row. `paymentSucceeded`: `paid`, a ledger charge line (`written_by` `provider_event`), the link ended `paid`, a fee or plan charge it names moved to charged, and for `saveCardForLater` a `saved_cards` row from `paymentMethodId` with the port request's customer (`saved_from_payment_id`). `paymentFailed` is one try on the page: the payment stays `pending` until another card pays it or its deadline closes it, so it never writes `failed`. `closePayLink` and `paymentCanceled`: `cancelled`, the link ended `closed`.
- **`refund`**: K-14's `paymentId` is the refunded payment's PaymentIntent, read from `payments.effect_id`, then `effects.provider_object_id`; for a double charge, which no payment asked for, it is the listed charge's `paymentId` (K-14 `listItem`). The result and `refundChanged` map onto `refunds.state`: `pending` and `requiresAction` are `pending`; `succeeded` is `succeeded`, with a refund line; `failed` and `canceled` are `failed`, with a `refund_failed` line only when a refund line was written before.
- **`listMoney`**: C-112's daily run and its findings; a listed object with no effect id gets no line and no refund (CQ-15).
- **Disputes** (`disputeOpened`, `disputeClosed`) write no row in v1 (open gaps); they find their payment by `chargeId` in `ledger_lines.stripe_object_id` or by `paymentId` in `effects.provider_object_id`.

### FS section 9, field by field

- FS line 731 `payment`: `id`, `booking_id`; `kind` (due now, deposit, full, rest, bill, card at DONE, pack, membership, extra) is `purpose` (A5-04: with several services, part paid now and part left is a deposit, all of it paid now the full price; "bill" and "card at DONE" are a rest by a pay link or by the saved card; "membership" is a plan charge), with fee and tip from K-14; `amount` is `amount_cents` with `currency`; `method` (card, bank transfer, cash, own way); `status` (pending, paid, failed, cleared) is `state`, cleared being paid for a bank transfer; `stripe_ref` is the K-07 effect (`effect_id`), whose `provider_object_id` holds the PaymentIntent, and the ledger line's `stripe_object_id` the charge; `at` is `asked_at` and `paid_at`; a fee's or plan charge's payment names it (`booking_fee_id`, `plan_charge_id`).

Inside a turn every write rides K-05's one commit per turn, which runs once per turn id (a repeated turn writes nothing twice); an effect's result, a provider event and a job are held by the unique keys above; a write from a page or the console with no turn waits for K-05's commit with no turn (QUESTIONS.md CQ-44). Every table is scoped by `business_id` (rule 5).

### Money (rule 9)

Every amount is integer minor units in `bigint` (cents for USD) with its three-letter currency, never a decimal (CQ-09; K-30 v1 "Money"; K-33 v1 rule 5): `'39.50'` as cents is refused by the type (22P02). Nothing is ever held on a card: a payment is taken at once (paid), waits (pending: a pay link, a bank transfer, a bank's check with the person present), fails or is cancelled; no state, purpose or column holds a hold or a later capture (RULES 3.2; D65; K-14 v1; refuse-hold-payment). All that is due now is one payment on one card (RULES 3.2), whose items put each part against its line. A ledger line's amount is positive and its kind says which way the money moved. Rosfield's fees are USD, as K-33's bill.

### Times (rule 9)

Every instant is `timestamptz` in UTC. Every day column (`memberships.started_on`, `renews_on`, `next_charge_on`; `plan_charges.period_start`, `period_end`, `due_on`; `rosfield_fees.month`) is a day in the business's zone (K-04 `businesses.time_zone`; CQ-05), and a plan charge by the visit takes the visit's day, read in the visit's zone when the visit is at the customer's place (K-30 rule 9). The reconciliation window is UTC by definition (arch 10 line 550: "03:00 UTC").

### Personal data

No column holds a person's name, contact, words, address or card details: persons are named by id, a card only by Stripe's opaque customer and payment-method ids (never 13 digits in a row: `saved_cards_no_card_number`, `ledger_lines_stripe_id`), and a Stripe customer holds no name or contact from Rosie (K-14 v1 default 12). A pay link's code, which lets anyone holding it pay (A9-11), is long and random (default 21) and kept only as its sha256 (`pay_links_code_hash_form`), outside the Tool API's grant (rule 6). So nothing here is sealed. Row-level security keeps every row to its business (`refuse-cross-business-payment-read`), and a Tool API row may name another part's row only of its own business (rule 5).

## Database rules beyond the table list

The table list's meta-schema has no place for these; the migrations must produce them too, and T-109 checks them.

1. **Same schema as core:** the 14 tables live beside K-04's and the other parts' tables, and their foreign keys name them by key with no schema prefix (`businesses.id`, `persons.id`, `bookings.id`, `open_questions.id`, `outbox_rows.id`, `effects.id`, `consents.id`, `services (id, business_id)`, `customers (business_id, person_id)`, `booking_lines.id`, `booking_visits (id, booking_id)`). The migration runs after K-05 v1.1's (`effects`) and the K-25, K-28, K-29 and K-30 migrations, and creates the tables before their foreign keys: `payments` and `saved_cards`, `payments` and `booking_fees`, and `ledger_lines` and `refunds` name each other.
2. **Extensions:** none beyond K-04's.
3. **Ids** are `text`, opaque strings code makes (CQ-09); Stripe's ids are kept as K-14 gives them, opaque.
4. **Versions:** the tables marked versioned have `version integer not null default 1`, raised by every update and compared and set by a commit or a result (arch 01 line 85; K-04 v1 rule 2). `payment_items`, `ledger_lines`, `rosfield_fees` and `reconciliation_findings` are only inserted and carry none.
5. **Row-level security per business** (arch 10 line 546; K-04 v1 rule 4): every table is scoped by `business_id`, read and written by a Tool API call only in its own business (`business_id = nullif(current_setting('rosie.business_id', true), '')`). Inside the part, composite foreign keys hold every row to its own business (`(x_id, business_id)` to `(id, business_id)`). Other parts' rows K-04, K-07, K-29 and K-30 key by `id` alone, so the Tool API's insert and update policy also requires, for each of `booking_id`, `booking_line_id`, `visit_id`, `effect_id`, `save_effect_id`, `open_question_id`, `reminder_outbox_row_id` and `terms_consent_id` the row carries: `(<col> is null or exists (select 1 from <table> r where r.id = <row>.<col> and r.business_id = <row>.business_id))` over `bookings`, `booking_lines`, `booking_visits`, `effects`, `open_questions`, `outbox_rows` and `consents`; their own row-level security limits the subquery to the call's scope, so a call can never attach a payment to another business's booking (42501). On `payments` the same insert and update policy also requires `(payments.method in ('cash', 'own_way') or payments.state <> 'paid')`: a Tool API write never makes a card or bank-transfer payment paid (42501); only the runtime side's effect result or provider event does (K-05 v1.1; RULES 3.14), while cash and the business's own way are recorded paid by the person who says so. The runtime side (unscoped, as K-04 rule 4 allows) holds the same by code, and reads unscoped only for: the effect results and provider events (C-109, C-044), the plan charge job, reconciliation (C-112), the monthly bill job reading `rosfield_fees` (C-108), the pay page's server finding a pay link by its code's hash before it knows the business (C-200), and the 45-day erasure.
6. **What may be written, by privilege** (both application roles unless named): no `DELETE` and no `TRUNCATE` on any of the 14 tables (42501; a trigger cannot stop a `TRUNCATE`, so only the grant does), and neither application role owns a money table or `money_set_once()` (as DL-110 (5) and DL-111 (2) hold for the event log), so neither can drop, alter or disable a table, its policy or its trigger. `ledger_lines`, `reconciliation_runs` and `reconciliation_findings`: the Tool API may only `SELECT`; the runtime side inserts (`ledger_lines` and `reconciliation_findings` are never updated: `refuse-ledger-update`, 42501), and updates a run only in `finished_at`, `charges_listed`, `refunds_listed` and `version`. Insert only, no `UPDATE`: `payment_items`, `rosfield_fees` (`refuse-rosfield-fee-update`, 42501). `UPDATE` of named columns only: `memberships` (`next_charge_on`, `status`, `paused_at`, `ended_at`, `ended_why`, `version`), `saved_cards` (`stripe_customer_id`, `stripe_payment_method_id`, `saved_at`, `replaced_at`, `removed_at`, `removed_why`, `version`), `payments` (`state`, `paid_at`, `failed_at`, `failed_why`, `cancelled_at`, `version`), `refunds` (`state`, `refunded_at`, `failed_at`, `marked_done_by_person_id`, `version`), `pay_links` (`given_via`, `ended_at`, `ended_why`, `version`), `packs` (`credits_used`, `version`), `credits` (`returned_at`, `returned_why`, `version`), `booking_fees` (`reminded_at`, `answer`, `answered_at`, `answered_by_person_id`, `answered_by_role`, `option_id`, `event_id`, `state`, `charged_at`, `dropped_at`, `version`), `plan_charges` (`reminder_outbox_row_id`, `state`, `skipped_why`, `charged_at`, `failed_at`, `skipped_at`, `version`). So no amount, payer, purpose, method, effect, fee or plan charge a payment names, reason, decider, price, period, terms, count total or pay link code is ever changed in place, and a Stripe customer id only goes from null to its value (rule 7). Both roles may `SELECT` every column of every table with one exception: the Tool API's grant on `pay_links` names every column except `code_sha256`, so only the runtime side reads a code's hash, and a Tool API read of `pay_links` names its columns (`refuse-pay-link-code-read`: the whole row, 42501).
7. **Set once, by one trigger function:** a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger named `<table>_set_once` on nine tables refuses any change to a listed column once it is not null (a listed array may only gain items), raising SQLSTATE 23514 with the trigger's name as the constraint. The lists, in this order:
   - `memberships`: `ended_at`, `ended_why`;
   - `saved_cards`: `stripe_customer_id`, `stripe_payment_method_id`, `saved_at`, `replaced_at`, `removed_at`, `removed_why`;
   - `payments`: `paid_at`, `failed_at`, `failed_why`, `cancelled_at`;
   - `refunds`: `refunded_at`, `failed_at`, `marked_done_by_person_id`;
   - `pay_links`: `given_via` (a place added, none removed), `ended_at`, `ended_why`;
   - `credits`: `returned_at`, `returned_why`;
   - `booking_fees`: `reminded_at`, `answer`, `answered_at`, `answered_by_person_id`, `answered_by_role`, `option_id`, `event_id`, `charged_at`, `dropped_at`;
   - `plan_charges`: `reminder_outbox_row_id`, `skipped_why`, `charged_at`, `failed_at`, `skipped_at`;
   - `reconciliation_runs`: `finished_at`, `charges_listed`, `refunds_listed`.

   With the CHECKs that hold each state to its times (`payments_state_times`, `refunds_state_times`, `pay_links_ended`, `credits_returned`, `booking_fees_state_times`, `plan_charges_outcome`, `memberships_status`), a payment once paid, failed or cancelled, a refund once failed, a pay link once ended, a credit once given back, a fee once charged or dropped, a plan charge once charged or skipped and a membership once ended never change (arch 09 line 457, "MONEY append-only"; RULES 3.14), and a plan charge once failed can only go on to charged, by its one pay link (A7-54). The function, which T-041's check used: `create function money_set_once() returns trigger language plpgsql as $$ declare col text; begin foreach col in array tg_argv loop if (to_jsonb(old) ->> col) is not null and (case when jsonb_typeof(to_jsonb(old) -> col) = 'array' then not coalesce((to_jsonb(new) -> col) @> (to_jsonb(old) -> col), false) else (to_jsonb(new) ->> col) is distinct from (to_jsonb(old) ->> col) end) then raise exception 'a money value is set once and never changed' using errcode = 'check_violation', constraint = tg_name; end if; end loop; return new; end $$;`, attached as `create trigger payments_set_once before update on payments for each row execute function money_set_once('paid_at', 'failed_at', 'failed_why', 'cancelled_at')`, and so on for each list.

8. **Sealed:** none (see "Personal data"). The 45-day delete (RULES 4.10; C-156) touches nothing here: no row holds a person's private words.
9. **Retention:** money records are kept 7 years with the booking (A6-10's rule; S13-08; FS line 754); no application role deletes a row (rule 6). A saved card's row stays after the card is removed from Stripe. The purge after 7 years is a job with no contract yet (open gaps).

a $2 is counted once per booking made through Rosie, never for a walk-in, a booking the business added, a plan charged monthly or work with the no-$2 switch on (A9-21), toward the person who gave the work, and its month is `counted_at`'s month in the business's zone;
