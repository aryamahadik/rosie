## What it defines

The customer record of FS line 727 (FS section 9 as the checklist, under Q-33's default: arch 09's shape) and the block of S11-62, following 02-contracts section 7 row K-28 and the keys `customer`, `reach_by` and `contact` of section 8. Arch 09 gives the "Customers" domain 7 tables and no names (line 465); K-04 core already holds the person, the roles, the handles, the phones and the pauses, so this part holds 2 (open gaps). Every table and column names its source in `customers.v1.tables.json`.

- **`customers`**: a business's record of one customer, per business and person: the name the business has on file; the contact on file, a mobile and an optional email, each a K-04 handle; the way they are reached; their contact preferences (the times they named, first name only, no voicemail); their age band; the owner's fee-free mark; when the list gained them. Sources: FS line 727; RULES 4.8, 4.10; S13-19; S13-45; A4-23, B4-25; A4-80, B4-92; A4-82, B4-93; A3-30, B3-08; S11-57; MC C2; Q-07.
- **`customer_blocks`**: a business's block of one customer, by their number or by their address for home visits, with who set it and when, from the AI or the portal, and its lift. Sources: S11-62; FS line 727 (blocked); FS section 6.2 (Customers).

### Which part owns what

Each fact has one home: this part names the others' rows by their keys and repeats none of their columns. The one overlap in kind, this business's name on file beside K-04's `persons.name`, is two facts (default 4).

- **K-04 core** (frozen, #55) owns the person (`persons`: their own name and language across Rosie, FS line 743), the customer role the guard lists (`person_roles`, role `customer`), every phone number and email address once (`handles`, one per address, and `person_handles`, which people write from which handle), each mobile's phone row across every business (`phones`: chat open, the Linq contact, and the stopped flag after STOP, MC C1), the paused flag for a person, phone or address (`pauses`, A3-48), the booking head (booker, person served, payer, private) and the sealing keys. A customer row names its person, its mobile and its email by K-04's keys (`persons.id`, `phones.handle_id`, `person_handles (person_id, handle_id)`), so it holds no phone number or email address at all; the number and the address are the K-04 handle's.
- **K-29 conversations and consent** (frozen, #59) owns every yes to texts as a `consents` row of kind `texts`, never edited, including the yes a business adds for a booking it makes (RULES 2.5; A3-30, B3-08): the person, the business, the mobile it covers, the booking, the read-back's exact words and hash, the owner's turn and channel. FS line 727's `texts_ok_at` is that row's `at`, and nothing here repeats it. K-29 also owns a STOP for every business, the revoked row of a contact switch (FS line 756) and the chat link the owner passes on for a booking the business adds (MC C9).
- **K-23 business setup** (frozen, #78) owns the business's own setup. Nothing here refers to its tables; this part's seeds use Lee Home Services, whose business head K-23's `seed-company-two-kinds` adds.
- **K-22 people and access** (not frozen: T-031) owns staff details and the contact change row of D75, D77 and D100 (02-contracts row K-22). Here a switch of where updates go is the customer row's new contact (a versioned update), beside K-29's two consent rows; nothing here refers to K-22.
- **K-26 rules** (frozen, #90): its `own_customers_only` rule (FS K10) reads this part's customer rows as the business's own customers, and its fee-free reasons stay notes; the mark itself is `customers.fee_free` (K-26 v1 README, open gaps).
- **K-30 bookings and changes** (F15, frozen with this part) owns the booking's values, its `reach` among them (K-01 v1 `bookingValues.reach`, SPEC-master 5e), and the secure link with the contact it went to (02-contracts section 8, `contact`): it names the customer's handle, never a copy of the number. **K-32 money** (F16) names a customer by `customers.id` for memberships, packs and credits (FS lines 737 to 739).

### FS line 727, field by field

- `id`, `business_id`: `customers.id`, `customers.business_id`.
- `name`: `customers.name`, the name this business has on file. K-04's `persons.name` is the person's own name across Rosie; a business's record is never copied from or to another business's (RULES 4.10).
- `mobile`: `customers.mobile_handle_id`, a K-04 handle with its `phones` row; required when the customer or their own AI books (MC C2; D103), which the reach card checks.
- `email`: `customers.email_handle_id`, a K-04 handle of kind email; optional (MC C2).
- `reach way`: `customers.way`: `texts`, `textsonly` or `email`, spelt as K-01 v1's `bookingValues.reach`; calls only is not offered (Q-07).
- `contact preferences`: `customers.contact_times` (their words), `first_name_only`, `no_voicemail` (S13-19).
- `age band`: `customers.age_band`: `18_or_older` or `under_18`, never an exact age (A4-80).
- `fee_free`: `customers.fee_free` (S11-57).
- `blocked`: an open `customer_blocks` row, by number or address (S11-62).
- `texts_ok_at`: K-29's `consents` row of kind `texts` for the booking (RULES 2.5; S13-32).

### The contact on file and the way (A4-82, B4-93; S13-19; MC C2; Q-07)

- The mobile is the handle of a K-04 `phones` row, so the phone row exists from the moment a customer's mobile is saved; that is where the Linq contact add is recorded (`phones.linq_contact_added_at`, Q-03: "Each mobile is added with contacts/add when it is saved"), and where the stopped flag that covers every business lives (MC C1). Both contacts are the person's own handles (`customers_mobile_own_fk`, `customers_email_own_fk` to `person_handles`), so a record never points at someone else's number.
- `customers_way_fits_contact` holds the way to the contact on file: texts and textsonly need the mobile, email needs the email, and a record with no contact has no way. A record with no contact is one the business booked with no mobile (A3-19), or one whose number staff took off in person (S13-19); Rosie sends them nothing, and the business reaches them its own way. An email on file gets every message whatever the way (RULES 4.8).
- `callsonly` is refused (`customers_way_known`; Q-07, MC C2) and `textsonly` works like texts while there is no voice (Q-07). A customer with only a landline is booked by the business (Q-07; A3-19) and keeps no landline here.
- A change on the same contact (texts to email, quiet times) is set at once on the customer's word in their own chat (A7-98); a new phone or email waits for the code typed back (D75, D77, D100; S13-45 row 3), and anyone's word can only make the preferences stricter (S13-19). These are the Tool API's rules (C-094), not constraints.

### The name and the age band

- The name is this business's record: as the customer's own AI sent it (A4-23, B4-25) or as the business booked them (A3-19, B3-08). A record on file is linked to a new conversation only when a proven contact and the name they type match (S13-45: "Have you booked here before? Under what name?"), and nothing on file (no name, no past times, no fee-free label) is used or hinted at before that (S13-45).
- The age band is the answer to "are you 18 or older?" (A4-80, B4-92), or the age the owner's read-back gave (A3-30, B3-08). A record that says `18_or_older` skips the question for its own person once a proven contact links it; `under_18` is asked again (A4-80). Nothing is kept about a child under 13 or a teen booking alone (RULES 4.7), and a text names a child's record only as RULES 4.7 allows ("Staff and owner texts name only the parent and the time or class"): the claim check (limit 7) reads `age_band` beside the column's audience.

### Blocks (S11-62)

- A block is by `number` (the customer's own mobile handle, `customer_blocks_phone_own_fk`) or by `address` (the street address for home visits, sealed under the customer's key as K-04's `pauses.address` is). `customer_blocks_customer_fk` holds a block to the business's own customer row: S11-62 blocks a customer.
- While a block holds, a new booking that gives that number, through the customer's own AI, the booking page or a text, or in flow B that address, hears only that the business can't take it, with no reason (S11-62). A number has at most one open block per business (`customer_blocks_one_open_number`); an address is sealed, so code unseals the business's open address blocks (`customer_blocks_open_address_idx`) and compares them.
- Lifting sets `lifted_at` and who; the row stays, so Rosie's team sees each block, lifted or not (S11-62: "in case it's a mistake"). Bookings already made, plans and credits are the action layer's (C-105), read back first (S11-62).
- A block is not a pause. A threat or a pro's UNSAFE pauses later visits with that customer and at that address (S11-62; A3-48), and that is K-04's `pauses`; a block refuses new bookings. Neither is K-23's PAUSE of new bookings or K-33's unpaid-bill pause.

## Database rules beyond the table list

The table list's meta-schema has no place for these; the migrations must produce them too, and T-105 checks them.

1. **Same schema as core:** both tables live beside K-04's tables (and K-23's and K-29's), and their foreign keys name core tables by K-04's keys (`businesses.id`, `persons.id`, `phones.handle_id`, `person_handles (person_id, handle_id)`, `turns.id`), so the references carry no schema prefix. No table here refers to K-23 or K-29, and none of theirs to these.
2. **Versions:** both tables are updated (a contact switched, the way or preferences changed, fee-free marked, a block lifted), so each has `version integer not null default 1`, raised by every update and compared and set by a commit (arch 01 line 85; K-04 v1 rule 2).
3. **Ids** are `text`, opaque strings code makes (CQ-09).
4. **Row-level security per business** (arch 10 line 546; K-04 v1 rule 4): both tables are scoped by `business_id`. A Tool API call sees and writes only its own business's customers and blocks, so one business never reads another's record of the same person (RULES 4.10: "never joined across businesses"). The runtime side reads unscoped only for jobs that work by person or handle: the dispatcher sending each row the person's way (C-124), the 45-day copy or delete (RULES 4.10), the reassigned-numbers check (RULES 6.5).
5. **No delete for the Tool API:** it may select, insert and update both tables, never delete (SQLSTATE 42501). A lifted block keeps its row (S11-62), and records are kept (RULES 4.10); the 45-day delete of a person runs on the runtime side (C-156).
6. **Sealed:** `customer_blocks.address`, under the key of `customer_blocks.person_id` in K-04's `sealing_keys` (arch 01 line 93; K-04 v1 rule 6). `customers.name` and `contact_times` are plain, as K-04's `persons.name` (K-04 v1 README, "Erasure of plain columns"; CQ-26's default), and the 45-day delete redacts or deletes them; the contacts are K-04 handles, so their addresses are erased where K-04 erases them.
7. **Rules across tables, kept by code** (not constraints): a customer row comes with a live K-04 `person_roles` row of role `customer` for the same person and business, written in the same commit (A4-23: "added to the business's customer list once booked"); `email_handle_id` names a handle of kind `email` (K-04 v1 gives `handles` no key on its kind); a mobile is required when the customer or their own AI books (MC C2), checked by the reach card's call, since a customer the business books may have none; a new booking's mobile, and in flow B its address, are checked against the business's open blocks before anything is saved (S11-62).

## Open gaps

- **Arch 09's count:** 7 tables for "Customers"; this part holds 2, since K-04 holds the person, the roles, the handles, the phones and the pauses. Arch 09 gives no names, so the count cannot be matched table for table (as K-29, K-33 and K-37 found for theirs).
- **Two names** (default 4): K-04's `persons.name` says "as they gave it or as a business saved it"; with this part a business's saved name is `customers.name`. Which name a person's own chat uses when they differ, and whether K-04's description should say so, is not in the sources. Proposed for QUESTIONS.md.
- **Contact times as words:** S13-19 and A3-50 give the times as the person's words ("Weekdays after 6pm"), and no source gives a form code can read, so the dispatcher cannot yet hold a message to them; v1 keeps the words for the business's own person, and Rosie's texts keep RULES 4.3's text hours. A readable form is a v1.1 column. Proposed for QUESTIONS.md.
- **The email handle's kind:** K-04 v1's `handles` has no key on `(id, kind)`, so `email_handle_id` cannot be held to kind `email` by a constraint; code checks it (rule 7). A K-04 minor version adding that key would let a K-28 minor version hold it.
- **A number that is no customer's:** S11-62 blocks a customer; a texter who never booked has no customer row, so v1 cannot block that number. Proposed for QUESTIONS.md.
- **Rosie's team seeing each block** (S11-62): K-37 v1's `review_items` has no kind for a block, so the team sees blocks through the event log (K-08) until a K-37 minor version adds one. Why a block was set is not stored: AI assistants hear no reason, and S11-62's rule ("never for who they are") is for the read-back.
- **Matching a blocked address:** sealed, so code unseals the business's open address blocks to compare; no match key is stored. A keyed hash for matching would be a v1.1 column if the count grows.
- **An organization that books** (A4-89: "An organization that books is the customer"): K-04's `persons` are people; v1 has no organization customer.
- **A landline** (MC C2's DD; Q-07): no landline is kept and no `callsonly` way is offered. If Jay takes calls only up, a landline column and a widened `customers_way_known` are a v1.1 change.
- **A child's own record and the family record** (A4-25: "The family's record on the secure page decides who may book and hear"): not in FS line 727; a child's details stay on the secure page (K-30's page answers). Whether a child served on a parent's booking gets a customer row is not in the sources.
- **Customer notes and records** (S11-61: "Sensitive notes stay out unless he ticks them"; "the plug-in's record rules"): not in FS line 727, so v1 holds none; an export reads these rows.
- **A payer who is no customer** (A4-55): has no customer row; the email for their pay link is a K-04 handle of their person, named by K-30 or K-32.
- **CQ-09's id forms:** ids are opaque text until the CTO rules.
